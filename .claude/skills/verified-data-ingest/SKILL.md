# Verified data ingest

The failure mode is never "I couldn't find the number". It's **writing a plausible number that is wrong**, at scale, into something customers read. A fabricated discount doesn't just misinform — it makes the product lie on your behalf.

## The cardinal rule

A fact is only ever **read** from its authoritative source. Never inferred, estimated, rounded to something nicer, carried forward from a stale value, borrowed from a sibling product, or generated by a model. If it can't be read, it is **UNREADABLE** — reported and left alone, never guessed.

Corollary: **no LLM ever authors a price, rating, review count, or expiry date.** Not "approximately", not "based on similar products". Those are the exact phrasings that ship falsehoods.

## Two-source rule

Nothing gets written unless a **second, independent read agrees exactly**. Independent means a different fetch path and a different parser — not the same HTML parsed twice.

This is not ceremony. Real rejections it produced in one session:

| Candidate | Second source said | Why it mattered |
|---|---|---|
| plate set → $69.99 | listing sells 5 weights, $69.99–$199.99 | $69.99 is the 10lb pair; catalog row is a *set* |
| bench → $159.99 | shop trades in **CAD** | would have invented a 51% discount |
| "sale ended" | page shows **$899 was $999** | would have deleted a live discount |
| mat → $148 | out of stock, no price shown | unverifiable ≠ confirmed |

Disagreement is a **refusal**, not a tie to break.

## Refusal taxonomy

Make "unreadable" specific — a bare failure count hides the fixable causes.

- `ambiguous-variants` — listing is priced per variant and the record doesn't say which. **Unanswerable until the schema records the variant.** This is a data-model gap, not a scraping gap.
- `currency-XXX` — source trades in a currency the destination isn't in. Always check; a bare number carries no units.
- `robots-disallow` / `tos-forbidden` — the source forbids automated reads. Use its official API or mark unreadable. Never route around it to hit a coverage target.
- `http-403` / `http-429` — bot protection or rate limiting. Different from "gone".
- `no-markup` — page has no machine-readable price.
- `http-404` — dead. Distinguish **moved** (relink) from **discontinued** (delist) from **never matched** (the record describes something the source doesn't sell in that form).

## Traps that manufacture false data

1. **Absence of evidence read as evidence.** "No compare-at price in the markup" means *you couldn't see one*, not that the sale ended. Only a source that can genuinely observe the field may conclude it's absent.
2. **Lowest-number-on-page.** Configurator and bundle pages expose accessory prices. Taking the minimum priced a $795 bench at $27.
3. **Content-type gates.** Endpoints often serve JSON as `text/javascript`. Gating on content-type silently drops your best source; parse defensively instead.
4. **Silent-success on empty.** An extractor returning nothing must never be classified "unchanged". A run that reads zero records is a **broken run**, and must exit non-zero — otherwise a dead selector reports a clean bill of health.
5. **Identity drift.** Same brand, different product. Relinking a "Competition Flat Bench" to an FID bench carries the old rating, review count and verdict onto a product they were never about. Worse than a 404.

## Writing back

- Automation opens a **pull request**; a human merges. Never write facts straight to main. That review is what stops a broken extractor rewriting every record.
- Only the source that can confirm *all* preconditions may auto-edit. Sources that can't verify currency/variants/compare-at get reported as needs-review and never become edits on their own.
- Publish the summary somewhere visible so a zero-read run is loud rather than looking clean.
- Attribute honestly. A retailer's own claim may be quoted as the retailer's. It may **not** be attributed to a review publication that never wrote it.

## When the schema forces a lie

If a required field can't be sourced for most records, the schema is the bug. Requiring a star rating on every product forces a choice between inventing ratings and not listing products. Make it optional (see `optional-field-migration`) rather than filling it in.
